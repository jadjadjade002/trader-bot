# R1/R2 development failure attribution

## Scope and result

This note uses only accepted development evidence for 2025-12-01 through 2026-06-01. R1 contributes 108 accepted configurations; R2 contributes ten accepted mode/preset runs. All 118 fail the frozen development gate (net > 0, net PF >= 1.20, at least 150 positions). R1 has many positive-net cells, but its best PF is 1.0896. R2's highest PF is 1.0656; two low-frequency families also miss the position floor. These results show weak and unstable net expectancy in the tested populations. They do not identify a single causal defect or support promotion.

## Evidence for weak expectancy

R1's accepted optimization rows aggregate net, gross profit/loss, position count and drawdown by configuration. Positive rows still have PF near 1: continuation max PF 1.0896 (1,797 positions), reclaim 1.0640 (1,728), breakout max PF 1.0509 (1,314). Breakout's best-net row is different: +$388.91, PF 1.0460, 1,762 positions. Every positive-net row therefore gives back most gross gains in gross losses. These cited rows have enough positions, so low PF is their binding failure, rather than the sample floor.

The R1 matched grid holds mode, stop width and entry-strength preset fixed while changing TP R. Across these matched cells, TP 1R is uniformly negative in all three families. TP3 produces the strongest groups: continuation 5/9 positive, mean net -$33.14; reclaim 7/9, +$209.69; breakout has positive cells only at wider stop settings, with no qualifying row. This repeatable association is consistent with short targets clipping occasional favorable follow-through, but it does not establish that TP distance caused the difference: each TP setting changes realized trade exits and subsequent signal availability, and the accepted grid has no per-trade exit or path attribution.

R2 fixes economics at SL 1.5 ATR, TP 2R, 60-bar maximum hold and break-even off. Across its ten development runs, five have positive net, yet best net PF is only 1.0656. The two high-frequency deeper-pullback presets return just +$0.045 and +$0.098 per position, with PF 1.0122 and 1.0264. Compression loses in both presets. Range-reversal and break-retest have only 56–75 positions and cannot clear the sample floor. The near-zero per-position returns in high-count rows indicate that gross winning and losing trade economics nearly cancel; extra-cost stress makes most small positive rows negative, though that stress is accounting sensitivity rather than an observed fee.

## Direction and exit aggregates

R2 accepted development records report BUY/SELL margins and exit-code margins separately. BUY net is negative in all ten runs; SELL net is positive in deeper-pullback, break-retest and exhaustion, and negative in compression and range-reversal. Across runs, exit code 4 totals 3,662 positions and -$20,191.51; code 5 totals 1,679 and +$19,988.05; code 3 totals 77 and +$82.45. Codes remain identifiers here; no semantic exit label is inferred.

These are marginal totals, not a matched side-by-exit cross-tab. Candidate mode changes which trades enter, so side totals also compare different entry populations. They cannot show that BUY selection is defective, that SELL is inherently better, or that one exit code causes the losses. R1 optimization data contains no direction or exit attribution at all. Its per-trade deal/path exports are absent for the optimization passes; accepted rows cannot say whether a target, stop or time exit dominates.

## Bounded causal hypotheses

1. **Payoff distance versus available excursion.** R1's TP1 losses and stronger TP3 group association suggest some entries need more room to realize follow-through. A competing explanation is that the apparent improvement comes from a different mix of exits and trade overlap. R2's sampled MFE/MAE paths can establish observed excursion while each position remained open, but cannot reconstruct intrabar first-hit ordering for an alternate target after the actual stop or time exit; lack of a sampled touch also does not prove a level was unreachable. R1 optimization rows have no such per-trade paths.
2. **Entry signal quality and follow-through may be mismatched.** R1 continuation and breakout require a trend-aligned impulse beyond a recent level; reclaim requires a trend-aligned EMA9 touch and close back across it. R2 uses trend-aligned pullback/compression/break-retest/exhaustion variants, except range-reversal which explicitly selects a flat M5 context. PF near one across many trades is consistent with weak conditional edge after costs and shared risk rules, but accepted aggregates cannot distinguish noisy entries from poor exit placement.
3. **Range geometry may conflict with fixed R targets.** A plausible, untested mechanism is that a fixed TP lies beyond nearby completed-bar structure in some entries, while a stop remains exposed to ordinary retracement; range reversal may have a different geometry again. No accepted development field measures entry-to-target distance against a preregistered prior-range boundary or subsequent path. This is a diagnostic question, not an explanation established by current data.
4. **Regime and direction mixture may dilute conditional expectancy.** The strategies gate on completed-bar trend/context, but accepted performance is not stratified by a frozen trend-persistence, volatility or chop definition. R2 BUY/SELL margins motivate a controlled check, not inversion or side filtering. No direction claim follows without comparing sides within the same signal/risk design and with adequate counts.

## What would test these explanations

Use a separately specified development-only attribution replay on the same six-month slice. Before examining outcomes, define completed-bar regime bins, range/target geometry measures, and exit-reason mapping from native deal reason fields. Export each position's mode, preset, direction, entry context, configured SL/TP, MFE/MAE, holding duration and exit reason. Report side-by-exit and regime-by-exit tables with counts, net and PF; compare only like-for-like cohorts where entry rules and risk settings match. For target-versus-range, evaluate geometry at entry and report realized path against both stop and target, without selecting a new TP from these results. Keep all results diagnostic; do not choose or rank a configuration using later-month outcomes or a retrospectively defined subgroup.

Current evidence establishes failed development gates and a few aggregate associations. It does not establish wrong BUY/SELL direction, a target-outside-range defect, a regime defect, or a causal explanation for weak expectancy.
